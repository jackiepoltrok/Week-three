This week we went to the Billings Estate to examine some gravestones. My group spent most of our time doing photogrammetry with Kavita. 
After playing around with the equipment, she sent us off to take photos of the smoke house for the purpose of creating a digital model of them. When she sent us off, she advised us to really pay attention to our process. What we were seeing, how we felt about it, what we were taking photos of, etc.
This made me think about the readings that we read for this week on digital archaeology ethics. In the Dennis reading, education of digital archaeology ethics was mentioned in detail. In universities right now, Dennis said many undergraduate students studying digital archaeology often do not have a practical body to oversee the ethics of their work or have professionals to consult with. While working with Kavita, I felt that if I needed to consult with someone over the ethics of my work, I could consult her which felt reassuring.
The reading also noted how problems occur when students are taught to use digital tools without being taught the accompanying ethical consideration of those tools. It is interesting to be present and participating in the changing world of a particular study like archaeology. Even the fact that a digital archaeology course is offered at Carleton is quite progressive to me. While reading this, it was reassuring to know that in this class, we are being taught about these ethics. And even in discussions in class and in the lectures, Professor Graham often mentions ethical considerations to keep in mind.
Overall, I thought the trip to the Billings Estate was a lot of fun. It was very cool to actually be out in the field, using the fancy photogrammetry equipment, and working with my peers.
